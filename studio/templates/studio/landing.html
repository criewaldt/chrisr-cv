→ misallocated capacity

→ slow cash collection

→ single point of failure

Systems that don't talk

The same data keyed into three places, staying wrong in at least one of them.

→ compounding errors

06  —  FAQ

The questions you're already asking.

What does a project typically cost?
It depends entirely on scope, so I won't publish a number that would be wrong for your situation. What I will do is give you a fixed price before any work starts — after the scoping call, in writing, with a date attached. If the number doesn't work, you've lost thirty minutes and nothing else.
How long does something like this take?
Most systems on this page reached a useful first version in four to eight weeks, then kept growing. You'll see working software weekly from the start, so you're never waiting on a big reveal to find out whether it's going well.
I'm not sure software is even the answer.
Then the scoping call is genuinely worth taking, because sometimes it isn't. A process change or an off-the-shelf tool is occasionally the better answer, and I'd rather tell you that than sell you a build you didn't need.
Who owns the code?
You do, outright. It's deployed to your infrastructure, under your accounts, and documented so another engineer can pick it up. No hostage-taking, no proprietary platform you can only leave by starting over.
What happens after it launches?
Your choice. Plenty of clients take the handover and run it themselves. Others keep me on a retainer for changes and support — that's how the systems on this page have stayed up. Neither is assumed, and you're not locked into either.
